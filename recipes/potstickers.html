Chinese Potstickers

Description

The word potsticker comes from the Chinese word wotip which literally means pot stick. Generally, wotip refers to pan fried dumplings, and this is just one of the thousands of varieties of dumplings found in cuisines all across the world. Even in just Chinese cuisine, you'll find dumplings with savory or sweet fillings, prepared in all sorts of methods. No one agrees on the exact origin of Chinese potstickers, but by one account, these came about when a chef of the Imperial Court accidentally left dumplings cooking in a wok, burning the bottom and claiming them as his own special creation.

This recipe is taken from Made With Lau.

Ingredients

  • 454g chicken
  • 4 stalks of celery
  • 454g cabbage
  • 28g ginger
  • 1 tablespoon of oyster sauce
  • 1 tablespoon of soy sauce
  • 1 tablespoon of fish sauce
  • 1 teaspoon of white pepper
  • 1 tablespoon of chicken bouillon powder
  • 1 teaspoon of sugar
  • 1 teaspoon of salt
  • 3 tablespoons of cornstarch
  • 2 tablespoons of water
  • 1 tablespoon of sesame oil
  • 2 tablespoons of oil
  • 1 pack potsticker wrappers
  • 1 tablespoon of white vinegar
  • 1 teaspoon of chili oil
  • 1/2 teaspoon of sugar

Steps

  1. Prepare the chicken:
    • Cut the chicken into thin slices. To make slicing easier, partially freeze the chicken beforehand.
    • Overlay the slices and cut them into thin strips. Rotate the strips 90 degrees and mince the chicken into small bits.
    • Set aside in a large bowl.
  2. Make the marinade:
    • In a small bowl, mix together the oyster sauce, soy sauce, fish sauce, white pepper, chicken bouillon powder, sugar, salt, cornstarch, and water.
    • Once fully combined, add the mixture to the minced chicken and knead until the meat is fully coated.
  3. Prepare the vegetables:
    • Mince the celery and cabbage, placing them in a medium-sized bowl.
    • Blanch the vegetables for one minute to remove any grassy taste, then drain and squeeze out excess water. This step ensures the filling won't be too wet, making it easier to wrap the potstickers.
  4. Combine filling ingredients:
    • Cut the ginger into small pieces similar to the chicken.
    • Mix the ginger with the vegetables and chicken, adding 1 tablespoon sesame oil.
    • Ensure the filling isn't too wet. If it is, drain any excess liquid or add more cornstarch. You can also refrigerate the filling to firm it up for handling.
  5. Set up your wrapping station: Have a bowl of water within reach, along with the filling and wrappers. Also, keep a tray nearby to place the wrapped potstickers.
  6. Wrap the potstickers:
    • Place a wrapper in your non-dominant hand with the unfloured side facing up.
    • Add 2 teaspoons of filling to the center of the wrapper and brush water along the edges.
    • Hold the unwrapped dumpling and fold it into a half-moon shape.
    • Starting from one side, fold small amounts of dough on top of one another to create pleats. Aim for about five evenly spaced pleats for a ruffled appearance.
  7. Cook the potstickers:
    • Heat oil in a pan over medium-high heat and arrange the potstickers evenly, ensuring they don't touch.
    • Pour in enough water to reach halfway up the potstickers and cover with a lid.
    • Cook for about 3-4 minutes until the water begins to boil.
    • Reduce the heat to low and continue cooking until almost all the water has evaporated.
    • Remove the lid and gently shift the potstickers to prevent sticking. Add a little more oil if needed.
    • Place the lid back on for another 30 seconds or until the oil is absorbed and the potstickers are crispy.
    • Check for doneness by lifting them up to see if the underside is golden brown.
    • Flip the potstickers over and cook for an additional minute.
    • Remove the lid and turn off the heat.
  8. Prepare the dipping sauce: In a small bowl, mix together the vinegar, chili oil, and sugar. Taste and adjust the flavors to your liking.
  9. Serve: Plate the potstickers on a large serving dish and eat!
